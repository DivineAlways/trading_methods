Beyond the ICC Method: Institutional strategies for higher velocity execution.

Speed vs. Structure

The ICC Method (Indication, Correction, Continuation) is a robust Market Structure strategy. It relies on H1 Fractals and structural shifts. It is powerful but inherently slower (swing/day trading).

To build a faster agent, you must move from Structure to Flow and Statistics. The two proposed strategies operate on sub-minute logic, requiring different architectural blueprints.

Strategy 1: Order Flow Imbalance (OFI)

Logic: Detects immediate aggressive buying or selling pressure in the Order Book (DOM).
Trigger: When Bid/Ask imbalance > 300% on a price level sweep.
Execution: M1 or Tick Charts. Exits are rapid (minutes).

Stop Loss tight (recent swing low). **System Constraints:** * **Language:** Python 3.10+ (Asyncio required). * **Data:** Websocket stream (Binance, Bybit, or OANDA v20 streaming). * **Speed:** Logic must execute under 200ms. **Development Protocol:** **Step 1: The Firehose (Async Data Layer)** * Implement `aiohttp` or `websockets` to ingest trade stream and orderbook snapshots. * Create a circular buffer (deque) to store the last 60 seconds of tick data. * *Verification:* Script outputs "Ticks per second" stats to console. **Step 2: The Imbalance Engine** * Calculate **Order Flow Imbalance (OFI)**: $OFI_t = (BidVol_t - BidVol_{t-1}) - (AskVol_t - AskVol_{t-1})$. * Implement a function `detect_absorption()`: High volume printed at a specific price level with price not moving. **Step 3: Execution Guard** * **Spread Filter:** Do not trade if spread > 0.5 pips. * **Volatility Filter:** Use standard deviation of tick returns. If vol is too low (dead market), pause. **Step 4: The Logic Loop** * Combine: If `OFI > Threshold` AND `Absorption == False` -> Trigger Buy.

Strategy 2: Statistical Mean Reversion (BB-RSI)

Logic: Price is elastic. When it stretches too far from the mean (Average), it snaps back.
Trigger: Price closes outside Bollinger Bands (2.5 StdDev) + RSI Extreme (>70 or <30).
Execution: M5 or M15 Charts. Reliable, quantitative, easier to code than Order Flow.
